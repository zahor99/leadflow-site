3 · Workstream 3 — Lead magnets & content plan

Two-magnet stack plus the diagnostic. Each entry: what it is, what content must be produced, source material, production owner, and the claim gate.

LM1 · exists · conversion asset, not content

CEO AI Operating Map

The meeting-generating offer — a structured diagnostic delivered from a call. Content to produce: none. Process to document: a one-page internal fulfillment SOP (intake → call → map template → delivery within X days) so ad-driven volume doesn't improvise. Owner: Gergely + Mo, 1 hour.

LM2 · exists as a page — verify the asset · English

The CEO's AI Leverage Blueprint (executive white paper)

The site promises immediate delivery by email. Verify with Mo: (a) the PDF actually exists and matches the promised outline (counsel before tools · AI/code/automation/humans division · first operational win · leadership questions); (b) where form submissions land; (c) whether delivery is automated. If the PDF is thin or missing, production plan: ~14 pages from the revamp's argument + the proof stories, drafted in 2 days, designed via the client-report pipeline, Mo reviews voice. This is the mid-funnel asset for UK campaigns A/B retargeting and email capture.

LM3 · new · German · the 12,100-searches/mo play

„Der EU AI Act für Geschäftsführer" — the CEO briefing

The asset the cheapest audience in the dataset downloads. 12–16 pages, German, executive register — a briefing, not a legal treatise.

  • Outline: 1) What the AI Act actually requires and when (timeline incl. current Digital-Omnibus state) · 2) Which obligations hit ordinary companies vs. AI vendors · 3) The GDPR × AI Act overlap — employee and customer data in AI tools · 4) The five decisions a CEO owns (not IT) · 5) The compliant-by-architecture pattern: in-tenant AI, anonymisation gates, human approval, audit trails · 6) A 90-day readiness checklist · 7) One anonymised worked example (the flagship engagement — wording cleared for identifiability, not just naming).
  • Source material advantage: a sourced GDPR/DPIA/AI-Act legal research package incl. Digital-Omnibus status was produced internally on 28 Aug — the legal skeleton exists; this is editing and translation, not research from scratch.
  • Production: English draft 2–3 days → professional German translation + native review ~1 week → designed PDF via the report pipeline, 1 day.
  • Claim gate: prominent "this is a briefing, not legal advice" disclaimer; no compliance guarantees; every legal statement carries its source and an as-of date. German-qualified legal review is a ship gate, not optional (per devil's-advocate review: RDG exposure + the AI Act timeline is in active flux) — the lawyer-review RFQ groundwork from Aug 28 can serve double duty.
LM4 · optional · fast follow

GDPR-compliant AI checklist (1 page, EN + DE)

A one-page architecture checklist ("10 questions before any AI tool touches company data"). Half a day to produce from the revamp's privacy-architecture section. Secondary magnet for compliance-cluster ads and a LinkedIn share asset.

LM5 · new · interactive tool — the highest-engagement magnet

CEO AI Readiness & Exposure Score (self-assessment)

An interactive 10–12 question self-evaluation a CEO completes in 3 minutes, scoring the company on two axes: AI leverage readiness (data accessibility, process documentation, system integration, ownership) and AI privacy exposure (shadow-AI usage, employee/customer data in tools, GDPR/AI-Act posture, vendor dependency). Instant on-screen score with a per-dimension gap readout; the detailed report + recommended first moves delivered by email (the capture).

  • Why it earns its place: it's the interactive twin of the Operating Map — the searcher typing "ai readiness assessment" or "ai risk assessment" (Campaign A keywords, both verified volume) gets exactly what they searched, self-qualifies through their own answers, and arrives at the meeting CTA with their gaps already named. Assessment tools typically out-convert static PDFs by a wide margin, and the answers themselves are qualification data for the sales call.
  • Question design: drawn from the Operating Map's own dimensions (§ site copy: where intelligence belongs / must not go / which constraint first) + the revamp's privacy architecture — so the tool teaches the CLH worldview while scoring. Every question answerable by a CEO without asking IT.
  • Scoring & output: four bands per axis (e.g. "Exposed / Aware / Governed / Architected"), each band mapping to 2–3 concrete first moves; the "Architected" description doubles as the CLH pitch. No fabricated benchmarks — scores compare against the rubric, not invented peer data.
  • Build: self-contained page on the site (P6, no backend needed beyond the form handler), EN first, DE version with Wave 2. ~1–2 days build once questions are agreed. CTA at result: "Your score suggests where to look — the Operating Map tells you what to do. Request it."
  • Claim gate: results framed as directional self-reflection, not audit findings; no compliance verdicts ("you are GDPR-compliant" is never an output).

Nurture sequence (post-download, LM2 + LM3 lists)

  1. Day 0 — delivery email: the PDF + one line on who wrote it (PhD-led firm, production AI at a multi-billion-dollar manufacturer).
  2. Day 3 — the worked example: how one European industrial company runs AI on live commercial data with zero personal records leaving its tenant (anonymised story).
  3. Day 8 — the employee-data angle: what shadow AI does with staff data, three questions to ask internally this week.
  4. Day 14 — the architecture email: tenant / gate / approval / audit-trail pattern in five paragraphs.
  5. Day 21 — the Operating Map offer: "if the briefing raised questions about your company specifically" → /strategy-session.

German list gets the sequence in German. Cadence stops on reply or booking. Emails drafted alongside the magnets (all five fit in a day once LM3 exists).

Compliance on our own funnel (non-negotiable given the positioning): double opt-in for the German list (German law + deliverability), a real unsubscribe in every send, privacy-policy update naming the ESP as processor, and Google Consent Mode v2 with a proper consent banner before any ad traffic lands. A privacy-first firm whose own funnel is sloppy on GDPR loses the pitch in the first click.
